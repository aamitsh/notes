01Parts of a circle

A circle is the collection of all points in a plane that are at a fixed distance (the radius) from a fixed point (the centre).

TermMeaning
ChordA line segment joining any two points on the circle.
DiameterA chord through the centre. It is the longest chord, and diameter = 2 × radius.
ArcA piece of the circle between two points. The smaller piece is the minor arc and the larger is the major arc. Two ends of a diameter cut the circle into two semicircles.
SegmentThe region between a chord and an arc: the minor segment (with the minor arc) and the major segment (with the major arc).
SectorThe region between two radii and an arc.
Congruent circlesCircles with equal radii. They fit exactly on top of each other.
Angle subtended by chord PQ at point R∠PRQ: the angle formed at R by joining R to both ends of the chord.
RememberA point is inside, on or outside a circle according to whether its distance from the centre is less than, equal to or greater than the radius.

Checkpoint

1. How many diameters can a circle have?

Show answer
Infinitely many. Every one of them passes through the centre.

2. A chord of a circle of radius 7 cm has length 14 cm. What kind of chord is it?

Show answer
A diameter, since 14 = 2 × 7. Its two arcs are semicircles.

3. The minor segment is bounded by which two things?

Show answer
The chord and the minor arc.
Got all 3 right?

02Equal chords and the angles they subtend at the centre

Theorem 9.1Equal chords of a circle subtend equal angles at the centre.

Proof idea: for equal chords AB and CD, compare △AOB and △COD: OA = OC and OB = OD (radii), and AB = CD (given). So △AOB ≅ △COD by SSS, and ∠AOB = ∠COD by CPCT.

Theorem 9.2 (converse)If the angles subtended by two chords at the centre are equal, the chords are equal.

Proof idea: OA = OC, ∠AOB = ∠COD and OB = OD, so the triangles are congruent by SAS, and AB = CD by CPCT.

Chords and arcs

  • If two chords are equal, their corresponding arcs are congruent. The converse is also true: congruent arcs have equal chords.
  • Congruent arcs subtend equal angles at the centre.
  • The angle subtended by an arc at the centre means the angle subtended by its chord: the minor arc subtends ∠POQ and the major arc subtends reflex ∠POQ.
Also true for congruent circlesEvery result in this section works for two different circles too, as long as they have equal radii (NCERT Exercise 9.1).

Checkpoint

1. Chords AB and CD of a circle are equal and ∠AOB = 84°. Find ∠COD and ∠OAB.

Show answer
∠COD = 84° (Theorem 9.1). △OAB is isosceles (OA = OB), so ∠OAB = (180° − 84°)/2 = 48°.

2. Which congruence rule proves Theorem 9.1, and which proves Theorem 9.2?

Show answer
9.1 uses SSS (all three sides known equal). 9.2 uses SAS (two radii and the included angle).

★ 3. A regular hexagon is drawn inside a circle with all six vertices on the circle. What angle does each side subtend at the centre, and how long is each side compared with the radius?

Show answer
The six sides are equal chords, so they subtend equal angles: 360° ÷ 6 = 60°. Each triangle formed with the centre has two radii and a 60° angle between them, so it is equilateral. Each side therefore equals the radius.
Got all 3 right?

03Perpendicular from the centre to a chord

Theorem 9.3The perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.
Theorem 9.4 (converse)The line drawn through the centre of a circle to bisect a chord is perpendicular to the chord.

Proof idea (9.4): join OA and OB. In △OAM and △OBM: OA = OB, AM = BM and OM is common, so they are congruent by SSS. That gives ∠OMA = ∠OMB, and since together they make 180°, each is 90°.

r² = d² + (l/2)²  ⇒  l = 2√(r² − d²),   d = √(r² − (l/2)²)

This single right triangle solves almost every numerical chord question: chord lengths, distances, parallel chords and common chords.

Worked example 1: chord lengthA chord is 5 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 13 cm. Half-chord = √(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12, so the chord = 24 cm.
Worked example 2: parallel chords (two cases)Chords of 6 cm and 8 cm in a circle of radius 5 cm. Distances from O: √(25 − 9) = 4 and √(25 − 16) = 3.
Same side of O: 4 − 3 = 1 cm apart. Opposite sides: 4 + 3 = 7 cm apart.
Worked example 3: common chord (NCERT 9.2 Q1)Circles of radii 5 cm and 3 cm, centres 4 cm apart. The line of centres is the perpendicular bisector of the common chord. Let the chord meet O₁O₂ at M with O₁M = x and half-chord h:
25 − x² = 9 − (4 − x)² ⇒ 25 = 9 − 16 + 8x ⇒ x = 4, so M = O₂ and h = √(25 − 16) = 3. Common chord = 6 cm.
Worked example 4: concentric circles (NCERT 9.2 Q4)A line cuts two concentric circles at A, B, C, D in order. Drop OM ⊥ to the line: M bisects both AD and BC, so AM = MD and BM = MC. Subtracting gives AB = CD.
Common trapTheorem 9.4 needs the chord to not be a diameter. A diameter is bisected by every line through O, and most of those lines are not perpendicular to it.

Checkpoint

1. Find the distance from the centre of a chord of length 16 cm in a circle of radius 10 cm.

Show answer
√(100 − 64) = √36 = 6 cm.

2. In a circle of radius 17 cm, two parallel chords are 30 cm and 16 cm long. Find both possible distances between them.

Show answer
Distances from O: √(289 − 225) = 8 and √(289 − 64) = 15. Same side: 15 − 8 = 7 cm. Opposite sides: 15 + 8 = 23 cm.

★ 3. Three girls stand on a circle of radius 5 m. Reshma–Salma = Salma–Mandip = 6 m. Find Reshma–Mandip (NCERT 9.2 Q5).

Show answer
SR = SM, so S is equidistant from R and M, and OS is the perpendicular bisector of RM, meeting it at P. Distance of O from RS = √(25 − 9) = 4, so area of △ORS = ½ × 6 × 4 = 12. The same area is ½ × OS × RP = ½ × 5 × RP, so RP = 4.8 and RM = 9.6 m.

★ 4. Three boys sit at equal distances on a circular park of radius 20 m, joined by toy-telephone strings. Find each string's length (NCERT 9.2 Q6).

Show answer
The three strings are equal chords, so they form an equilateral triangle and each subtends 120° at O. Dropping a perpendicular from O to one string gives a 30°–60°–90° triangle: distance = 20/2 = 10 m, half-string = √(400 − 100) = 10√3. Each string = 20√3 m.
Got all 4 right?

04Equal chords and their distances from the centre

The distance of a line from a point is the length of the perpendicular from the point to the line, which is the shortest segment joining them. If the point lies on the line, the distance is zero.

Theorem 9.5Equal chords of a circle (or of congruent circles) are equidistant from the centre (or centres).
Theorem 9.6 (converse)Chords equidistant from the centre of a circle are equal in length.

From l = 2√(r² − d²): the nearer a chord is to the centre, the longer it is. The diameter is at distance 0 and is the longest chord.

Worked example (NCERT Example 1)Two chords AB and CD intersect at E, and make equal angles with the diameter PQ through E. Draw OL ⊥ AB and OM ⊥ CD. In △OLE and △OME: ∠OLE = ∠OME = 90°, ∠LEO = ∠MEO (given) and OE is common, so they are congruent by AAS. Then OL = OM, so AB = CD by Theorem 9.6.
Worked example (NCERT 9.2 Q2 and Q3): equal chords that crossEqual chords AB and CD meet at E inside the circle. Then OL = OM (Theorem 9.5), and right △OLE ≅ △OME (RHS, OE common). This gives:
• EL = EM and AL = CM, so AE = AL + LE = CM + ME = CE, and likewise BE = DE. The segments are equal.
• ∠OEL = ∠OEM, so OE makes equal angles with the two chords.

Checkpoint

1. Two chords of a circle are at distances 3 cm and 5 cm from the centre. Which one is longer?

Show answer
The one at 3 cm, because it is nearer the centre.

2. Is the converse "chords equidistant from the centre are equal" true for two circles of different radii?

Show answer
No. It holds only for the same circle or congruent circles. For example, chords 3 cm from the centres of circles of radius 5 cm and 10 cm have lengths 8 cm and 2√91 cm.

★ 3. Two equal chords of length 16 cm in a circle of radius 10 cm cross at right angles at E. Find OE.

Show answer
Each chord is 6 cm from O. With OL ⊥ AB and OM ⊥ CD and AB ⊥ CD, OLEM is a rectangle with OL = OM = 6, i.e. a square. OE is its diagonal = 6√2 cm.
Got all 3 right?

05Angle at the centre is double the angle on the circle

Theorem 9.7: the most-used result in the chapterThe angle subtended by an arc at the centre is double the angle subtended by it at any point on the remaining part of the circle.

Proof outline: join AO and extend it to B. In △OAQ, OA = OQ, so ∠OAQ = ∠OQA. The exterior angle ∠BOQ = ∠OAQ + ∠OQA = 2∠OAQ. Similarly ∠BOP = 2∠OAP. Adding gives ∠POQ = 2∠PAQ.

The theorem has three cases: arc PQ is a minor arc, a semicircle, or a major arc. For a major arc, the centre angle is the reflex angle: reflex ∠POQ = 2∠PAQ.

Worked example (NCERT 9.3 Q1)∠BOC = 30° and ∠AOB = 60°, D on the circle off arc ABC. ∠AOC = 90°, so ∠ADC = ½ × 90° = 45°.
Worked example (NCERT 9.3 Q3)P, Q, R on the circle, Q on minor arc PR, ∠PQR = 100°. Q sees the major arc PR, so reflex ∠POR = 200° and ∠POR = 160°. △OPR is isosceles, so ∠OPR = (180° − 160°)/2 = 10°.
Worked example: chord equal to the radius (NCERT 9.3 Q2)△OAB is equilateral, so ∠AOB = 60°. On the major arc the angle is 30°; on the minor arc it is 180° − 30° = 150°.
Common trap"Double" works only when the point on the circle is on the other side of the chord from the arc being measured. A point on the minor arc sees the major arc, so use the reflex angle there, or use the 180° rule from Section 08.

Checkpoint

1. ∠AOB = 130° and C is on the major arc. Find ∠ACB.

Show answer
65°.

2. In the same circle, D is on the minor arc AB. Find ∠ADB.

Show answer
Reflex ∠AOB = 230°, so ∠ADB = 115° (also 180° − 65°).

★ 3. Triangle ABC is inscribed in a circle with centre O inside the triangle. ∠OBC = 20°. Find ∠BAC.

Show answer
△OBC is isosceles, so ∠BOC = 180° − 40° = 140°, and ∠BAC = 70°.
Got all 3 right?

06Angles in the same segment and the angle in a semicircle

Theorem 9.8Angles in the same segment of a circle are equal.

Why: each of them is half of the same centre angle (Theorem 9.7).

CorollaryThe angle in a semicircle is a right angle (90°).

Why: the centre angle of a semicircle is a straight angle, 180°, so the angle on the circle is 180° ÷ 2 = 90°.

Worked example (NCERT Example 2)AB is a diameter, CD is a chord equal to the radius, and AC and BD extended meet at E. △OCD is equilateral, so ∠COD = 60° and ∠CBD = 30°. ∠ACB = 90° (semicircle), so ∠BCE = 90°. In △BCE, ∠CEB = 90° − 30° = 60°.
Worked example (NCERT Example 4 and 9.3 Q10)Two circles meet at A and B, and AD and AC are diameters. Then ∠ABD = ∠ABC = 90° (semicircles), which add up to 180°, so D, B, C are collinear. Likewise, circles drawn on two sides of a triangle as diameters meet on the third side.
Spotting tipWhenever a figure has a diameter, look for a 90° angle at every point of the circle joined to both of its ends.

Checkpoint

1. In Fig 4 (left), ∠PAQ = 38°. Find ∠PCQ.

Show answer
38° (same segment).

2. AB is a diameter and C is on the circle with ∠CAB = 35°. Find ∠CBA.

Show answer
∠ACB = 90°, so ∠CBA = 180° − 90° − 35° = 55°.

★ 3. AB is a diameter, C is on the circle, and the bisector of ∠ACB meets the circle again at D. Find ∠ABD.

Show answer
∠ACB = 90°, so ∠ACD = 45°. ∠ABD = ∠ACD (same segment AD) = 45°, wherever C is.
Got all 3 right?

07Concyclic points

Points that lie on the same circle are called concyclic.

Theorem 9.9 (converse of 9.8)If a line segment joining two points subtends equal angles at two other points lying on the same side of the line containing the segment, the four points are concyclic.

Proof idea (by contradiction): draw the circle through A, C, B. If it misses D, it cuts AD (or AD extended) at E. Then ∠AEB = ∠ACB = ∠ADB, but the exterior angle of △BED can't equal its interior opposite angle unless E = D.

Worked example (NCERT 9.3 Q11)ABC and ADC are right triangles with common hypotenuse AC. Since ∠ABC = ∠ADC = 90°, B and D lie on the circle with AC as diameter. So A, B, C, D are concyclic, and ∠CAD = ∠CBD (same segment CD).
Worked example: feet of altitudesIn △ABC, BE and CF are altitudes. ∠BEC = ∠BFC = 90°, so B, C, E, F are concyclic (BC subtends equal angles at E and F, on the same side).
Common trapThe two points must be on the same side of the segment. If they are on opposite sides, use the cyclic-quadrilateral test instead (angles adding up to 180°).

Checkpoint

1. ∠ACB = ∠ADB = 50°, with C and D on the same side of AB. Are A, B, C, D concyclic?

Show answer
Yes, by Theorem 9.9.

★ 2. In the situation of Q1, ∠CAD = 25°. Find ∠CBD.

Show answer
The points are concyclic, so ∠CBD = ∠CAD = 25° (same segment CD).
Got both right?

08Cyclic quadrilaterals

A quadrilateral is cyclic if all four of its vertices lie on a circle.

Theorem 9.10The sum of either pair of opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral is 180°: ∠A + ∠C = 180° and ∠B + ∠D = 180°.
Theorem 9.11 (converse)If the sum of a pair of opposite angles of a quadrilateral is 180°, the quadrilateral is cyclic.

Useful consequences

ResultReason
Exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral = interior opposite angle∠CBE = 180° − ∠ABC = ∠ADC
A cyclic parallelogram is a rectangle (NCERT 9.3 Q12)Opposite angles are equal and add up to 180°, so each is 90°.
An isosceles trapezium is cyclic (NCERT 9.3 Q8)Its base angles are equal, so the co-interior angles give ∠A + ∠C = 180°.
If the diagonals of a cyclic quadrilateral are diameters, it is a rectangle (9.3 Q7)Each vertex angle is an angle in a semicircle.
A rhombus (that is not a square) is not cyclicIts opposite angles are equal but do not add up to 180°.
The quadrilateral formed by the angle bisectors of any quadrilateral is cyclic (NCERT Example 5)Opposite angles are 180° − ½(∠A + ∠B) and 180° − ½(∠C + ∠D), which add up to 180°.
Worked example (NCERT Example 3)ABCD is cyclic, ∠DBC = 55° and ∠BAC = 45°. ∠CAD = ∠DBC = 55° (same segment), so ∠BAD = 100° and ∠BCD = 180° − 100° = 80°.
Worked example (NCERT 9.3 Q6)∠DBC = 70°, ∠BAC = 30°, and AB = BC; find ∠ECD. ∠BAD = 30° + 70° = 100°, so ∠BCD = 80°. AB = BC gives ∠BCA = ∠BAC = 30°, so ∠ECD = 80° − 30° = 50°.
Worked example (NCERT 9.3 Q5)AC and BD meet at E, ∠BEC = 130° and ∠ECD = 20°. ∠BEC is the exterior angle of △DEC, so ∠EDC = 130° − 20° = 110°. Then ∠BAC = ∠BDC = 110° (same segment).
Worked example (NCERT 9.3 Q9)Two circles meet at B and C, and lines ABD and PBQ pass through B. ∠ACP = ∠ABP (same segment in circle 1), ∠ABP = ∠QBD (vertically opposite), and ∠QBD = ∠QCD (same segment in circle 2). So ∠ACP = ∠QCD.
Common trapThe angles that add up to 180° are opposite angles, not adjacent ones. In ABCD, those are A with C and B with D.

Checkpoint

1. ABCD is cyclic and ∠B = 68°. Find ∠D.

Show answer
112°.

2. In cyclic ABCD, ∠A = (2x + 4)°, ∠B = (y + 3)°, ∠C = (2y + 10)° and ∠D = (4x − 5)°. Find x and y.

Show answer
∠A + ∠C = 180° gives x + y = 83. ∠B + ∠D = 180° gives 4x + y = 182. So x = 33, y = 50, and the angles are 70°, 53°, 110°, 127°.

★ 3. In cyclic ABCD, AB is extended to E and ∠CBE = 105°. Find ∠ADC.

Show answer
The exterior angle equals the interior opposite angle, so ∠ADC = 105°.

★ 4. Prove that a cyclic trapezium (AB ∥ DC) has equal non-parallel sides.

Show answer
AB ∥ DC gives ∠A + ∠D = 180°. Cyclic gives ∠A + ∠C = 180°. So ∠C = ∠D, the base angles at DC are equal, and therefore AD = BC.
Got all 4 right?

Two intersecting circlesThe line of centres is the perpendicular bisector of the common chord

Radii OA and OB in a triangleThe triangle is isosceles, so the base angles are equal
Worked example: multi-stepTriangle ABC is inscribed with O inside it, ∠OBC = 20° and ∠OCA = 30°. Find ∠OAB.
∠BOC = 140°, so ∠BAC = 70°. ∠AOC = 120°, so ∠ABC = 60°. Then ∠ACB = 50°, so ∠AOB = 100° and ∠OAB = (180° − 100°)/2 = 40°.

Checkpoint

★ 1. ABCD is cyclic and AB is a diameter. ∠ADC = 130°. Find ∠BAC.

Show answer
∠ABC = 180° − 130° = 50°, and ∠ACB = 90° (semicircle). So ∠BAC = 40°.

★ 2. In acute △ABC, ∠B = 65°, and BE, CF are altitudes. Find ∠AEF.

Show answer
B, C, E, F are concyclic (∠BEC = ∠BFC = 90°). In cyclic quadrilateral BCEF, the exterior angle ∠AEF equals the interior opposite angle ∠FBC = 65°.

!!Common traps before the test

  • Minor or major arc? The angle at a point on the minor arc is obtuse; on the major arc it is acute. The two add up to 180°.
  • Reflex angle. When the point on the circle is on the minor arc, the centre angle to double back from is the reflex angle (360° − ∠AOB).
  • Parallel chords have two cases. Same side: subtract the distances. Opposite sides: add them. If the side isn't stated, consider both.
  • Half the chord, not the whole chord. In r² = d² + (l/2)², use half the chord length.
  • The diameter exception. "A line through O that bisects a chord is perpendicular to it" fails for a diameter.
  • Same side matters. Equal angles prove concyclicity only when the two points are on the same side of the segment.
  • Opposite, not adjacent. Only opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°.
  • Rhombus and parallelogram. They are not cyclic unless they are a square or a rectangle.
  • Radii make isosceles triangles. Use equal base angles every time two radii appear in a triangle.
  • Write the reason. In board answers, write the theorem in brackets after each step: (angle in a semicircle), (same segment), (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral), (CPCT).
