    01Parallelograms and their properties

    A quadrilateral has four sides, four angles and four vertices; its angles add up to 360°. A parallelogram is a quadrilateral in which both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.

    Theorem 8.1. A diagonal of a parallelogram divides it into two congruent triangles.
    Proof idea: in △ABC and △CDA, ∠BCA = ∠DAC and ∠BAC = ∠DCA (alternate angles), and AC is common. So △ABC ≅ △CDA by ASA.

    Property of a parallelogramConverse (a test for a parallelogram)
    Opposite sides are equal (Theorem 8.2)If each pair of opposite sides of a quadrilateral is equal, it is a parallelogram (8.3)
    Opposite angles are equal (8.4)If each pair of opposite angles is equal, it is a parallelogram (8.5)
    The diagonals bisect each other (8.6)If the diagonals bisect each other, it is a parallelogram (8.7)
    • Adjacent angles are supplementary: ∠A + ∠B = 180°, because AD ∥ BC and AB is a transversal.
    • Diagonals bisect each other means OA = OC and OB = OD. It does not mean the diagonals are equal.
    • One pair of opposite sides that is both equal and parallel is also enough to make a parallelogram. This is used in the proof of the Mid-point Theorem.

    Quick check

    1. In a parallelogram ABCD, ∠A = 65°. Find the other three angles.
      Show answer
      ∠C = 65°, ∠B = ∠D = 115°.
    2. The diagonals of a parallelogram meet at O, with OA = 4 cm and OB = 3 cm. Find AC and BD.
      Show answer
      AC = 8 cm, BD = 6 cm.
    3. In a parallelogram, ∠A = (2x + 15)° and ∠B = (3x – 10)°. Find the angles.
      Show answer
      Adjacent angles: 5x + 5 = 180, so x = 35. ∠A = 85°, ∠B = 95°.
    4. A quadrilateral has AB = CD = 5 cm and BC = DA = 7 cm. Is it a parallelogram?
      Show answer
      Yes, by Theorem 8.3 (each pair of opposite sides is equal).
    5. ★ In a parallelogram ABCD with AB = 9 cm and AD = 5 cm, the bisector of ∠A meets DC at E. Find EC.
      Show answer
      ∠DEA = ∠EAB (alternate angles) = ∠DAE, so DE = AD = 5 cm. EC = 9 – 5 = 4 cm.

    02Rectangle, rhombus and square

    • Rectangle: a parallelogram in which one angle is a right angle. Then every angle is 90°.
    • Rhombus: a parallelogram with all four sides equal.
    • Square: a rectangle with all sides equal. It is both a rectangle and a rhombus.
    DiagonalsParallelogramRectangleRhombusSquare
    bisect each otherYesYesYesYes
    are equalNot alwaysYesNot alwaysYes
    are perpendicularNot alwaysNot alwaysYesYes

    Each row also works backwards ("and vice-versa"): a parallelogram with equal diagonals is a rectangle; a parallelogram with perpendicular diagonals is a rhombus; with both, it is a square.

    Results from the chapter's examples and exercises

    • The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular. (△AOD ≅ △COD by SSS, so ∠AOD = ∠COD = 90°.)
    • If a diagonal of a parallelogram bisects one of its angles, the parallelogram is a rhombus. In a rhombus each diagonal bisects the angles at its ends.
    • The bisectors of the angles of a parallelogram form a rectangle.
    • When a transversal cuts two parallel lines, the bisectors of the interior angles form a rectangle.
    • In a trapezium ABCD with AB ∥ CD and AD = BC: ∠A = ∠B, ∠C = ∠D, and the diagonals are equal.
    • If a diagonal of a rectangle bisects the angles at its ends, the rectangle is a square.
    • △ABC is isosceles with AB = AC. If AD bisects the exterior angle PAC and CD ∥ AB, then ABCD is a parallelogram.
    Worked example. The diagonals of a rhombus are 16 cm and 12 cm. They bisect each other at right angles, so each side is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs 8 cm and 6 cm: side = 10 cm.

    Quick check

    1. The diagonals of a rhombus are 10 cm and 24 cm. Find its side.
      Show answer
      √(52 + 122) = 13 cm.
    2. The diagonals of a rectangle ABCD meet at O and ∠AOB = 50°. Find ∠OAB and ∠OAD.
      Show answer
      OA = OB, so ∠OAB = (180° – 50°) ÷ 2 = 65°. ∠OAD = 90° – 65° = 25°.
    3. A parallelogram has equal diagonals. What is it?
      Show answer
      A rectangle.
    4. True or false: (i) every square is a rhombus, (ii) every rectangle is a square, (iii) the diagonals of a rectangle are perpendicular.
      Show answer
      (i) True. (ii) False. (iii) False in general; true only when the rectangle is a square.
    5. ★ ABCD is a rhombus with ∠A = 60° and side 8 cm. Find the diagonal BD.
      Show answer
      AB = AD and ∠A = 60°, so △ABD is equilateral and BD = 8 cm.
    Got all 5 right?

    03The Mid-point Theorem

    Theorem 8.8: EF ∥ BC and EF = ½ BCthe line segment joining the mid-points of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and half of it
    Theorem 8.9 (converse): a line through the mid-point of one side, parallel to another side, bisects the third side

    Proof idea for 8.8: draw CD ∥ BA to meet EF produced at D. Then △AEF ≅ △CDF (ASA), so EF = DF and DC = AE = BE. BCDE has BE equal and parallel to DC, so it is a parallelogram. Hence EF ∥ BC and EF = ½ ED = ½ BC.

    Proof idea for 8.9: E is the mid-point of AB, and the line through E parallel to BC meets AC at F. Draw CD ∥ BA to meet this line at D. BCDE has both pairs of opposite sides parallel, so it is a parallelogram and DC = BE = AE. Then △AEF ≅ △CDF (AAS), so AF = CF.

    Results that follow

    • Four congruent triangles: joining the mid-points D, E, F of the sides of △ABC divides it into four congruent triangles. The perimeter of △DEF is half the perimeter of △ABC.
    • Mid-points of any quadrilateral: joining them in order always gives a parallelogram. Its sides are parallel to the diagonals and half as long.
    • Mid-points of a rectangle give a rhombus (equal diagonals). Mid-points of a rhombus give a rectangle (perpendicular diagonals).
    • Equal intercepts: if three parallel lines cut equal intercepts on one transversal, they cut equal intercepts on any other transversal.
    • Right triangle: the mid-point of the hypotenuse is equidistant from all three vertices. If the triangle is right-angled at C and M is the mid-point of AB, then CM = MA = ½ AB.
    • In a parallelogram ABCD with E, F the mid-points of AB and CD, the segments AF and EC trisect the diagonal BD.

    Quick check

    1. In △ABC, D and E are the mid-points of AB and AC, and BC = 14 cm. Find DE.
      Show answer
      DE = 7 cm, and DE ∥ BC.
    2. The sides of a triangle are 7 cm, 9 cm and 12 cm. Find the perimeter of the triangle formed by joining the mid-points of its sides.
      Show answer
      ½ × 28 = 14 cm.
    3. A right triangle has legs 5 cm and 12 cm. Find the distance from the mid-point of the hypotenuse to the right-angle vertex.
      Show answer
      Hypotenuse = 13 cm, so the distance is 6.5 cm.
    4. A rectangle is 6 cm by 8 cm. What figure is formed by joining the mid-points of its sides, and what is its perimeter?
      Show answer
      A rhombus. Each side is half a diagonal: ½ × 10 = 5 cm. Perimeter = 20 cm.
    5. ★ ABCD is a trapezium with AB ∥ DC, AB = 10 cm and DC = 6 cm. E and F are the mid-points of AD and BC. Find EF.
      Show answer
      Let EF meet the diagonal BD at G. In △DAB, EG = ½ AB = 5 cm. In △BCD, GF = ½ DC = 3 cm. So EF = 8 cm.
    6. ★ In △ABC, D is the mid-point of BC and E is the mid-point of AD. BE produced meets AC at F. Show that AF = ⅓ AC.
      Show answer
      Draw DG ∥ BF, meeting AC at G. In △ADG, E is the mid-point of AD and EF ∥ DG, so AF = FG. In △CBF, D is the mid-point of BC and DG ∥ BF, so FG = GC. Hence AF = FG = GC = ⅓ AC.
    Got all 6 right?

    04How to prove what a quadrilateral is

    To prove it is a ...Show any one of these
    ParallelogramBoth pairs of opposite sides are parallel; or both pairs of opposite sides are equal; or both pairs of opposite angles are equal; or the diagonals bisect each other; or one pair of opposite sides is equal and parallel
    RectangleA parallelogram with one right angle; or a parallelogram with equal diagonals
    RhombusA parallelogram with two adjacent sides equal; or with perpendicular diagonals; or in which a diagonal bisects a vertex angle
    SquareA rectangle with two adjacent sides equal; or a rhombus with one right angle

    Tools used again and again

    • Parallel sides give equal alternate angles and supplementary co-interior angles.
    • Congruent triangles (SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, RHS), followed by CPCT for the equal parts.
    • A diagonal is the usual construction: it splits the figure into two triangles to compare.
    • Mid-points suggest the Mid-point Theorem or its converse. Join a diagonal to create the triangles.
    • Write the reason beside every step. In CBSE marking, reasons carry marks.
    Worked example. The diagonal AC of a parallelogram ABCD bisects ∠A. Show that ABCD is a rhombus.
    ∠DAC = ∠BAC (given). ∠DAC = ∠BCA (alternate angles, AD ∥ BC). So ∠BAC = ∠BCA, which gives AB = BC (sides opposite equal angles). A parallelogram with two adjacent sides equal is a rhombus.

    Quick check

    1. The diagonals of a quadrilateral bisect each other at right angles. What is it?
      Show answer
      A rhombus (a parallelogram with perpendicular diagonals).
    2. Why is 'one pair of opposite sides parallel and the other pair equal' not enough to prove a parallelogram?
      Show answer
      A trapezium with equal non-parallel sides (AB ∥ CD, AD = BC) satisfies this but is not a parallelogram.
    3. All four angles of a quadrilateral are equal. Prove it is a rectangle.
      Show answer
      Each angle is 360° ÷ 4 = 90°. Opposite angles are equal, so it is a parallelogram (Theorem 8.5), and it has a right angle.
    4. ★ The diagonals of a parallelogram ABCD meet at O. A line through O meets AB at P and CD at Q. Show that OP = OQ.
      Show answer
      In △AOP and △COQ: ∠OAP = ∠OCQ (alternate angles), OA = OC, ∠AOP = ∠COQ (vertically opposite). So the triangles are congruent (ASA) and OP = OQ.
    Got all 4 right?

    05Proofs of every theorem and result

    CBSE papers ask for these proofs. For each one, draw the figure, write what is given and what is to be proved, then give the steps with reasons. The key steps are listed here.

    The nine theorems

    TheoremKey steps of the proof
    8.1 A diagonal divides a parallelogram into two congruent trianglesIn △ABC and △CDA: ∠BCA = ∠DAC and ∠BAC = ∠DCA (alternate angles), AC = CA (common). Congruent by ASA.
    8.2 Opposite sides of a parallelogram are equalFrom 8.1, △ABC ≅ △CDA. By CPCT, AB = DC and AD = BC.
    8.3 If each pair of opposite sides is equal, it is a parallelogramDraw AC. △ABC ≅ △CDA (SSS). So ∠BAC = ∠DCA, giving AB ∥ DC, and ∠BCA = ∠DAC, giving BC ∥ AD.
    8.4 Opposite angles of a parallelogram are equalFrom △ABC ≅ △CDA, ∠B = ∠D. Using the diagonal BD in the same way, ∠A = ∠C.
    8.5 If each pair of opposite angles is equal, it is a parallelogram∠A = ∠C and ∠B = ∠D, and the four angles add to 360°, so ∠A + ∠B = 180° and ∠A + ∠D = 180°. Co-interior angles are supplementary, so AD ∥ BC and AB ∥ DC.
    8.6 The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each otherIn △AOB and △COD: ∠OAB = ∠OCD and ∠OBA = ∠ODC (alternate angles), AB = CD (opposite sides). Congruent by ASA, so OA = OC and OB = OD.
    8.7 If the diagonals bisect each other, it is a parallelogramOA = OC, OB = OD and ∠AOB = ∠COD (vertically opposite), so △AOB ≅ △COD (SAS). Then ∠ABO = ∠CDO, giving AB ∥ CD. Similarly BC ∥ AD.
    8.8 Mid-point TheoremE, F are the mid-points of AB, AC. Draw CD ∥ BA to meet EF produced at D. △AEF ≅ △CDF (ASA), so EF = DF and DC = AE = BE. BCDE is a parallelogram, so EF ∥ BC and EF = ½ ED = ½ BC.
    8.9 Converse of the Mid-point TheoremE is the mid-point of AB and the line through E parallel to BC meets AC at F. Draw CD ∥ BA to meet it at D. BCDE is a parallelogram, so DC = BE = AE. △AEF ≅ △CDF (AAS), so AF = CF.

    The seven solved examples

    ResultKey steps of the proof
    Example 1. Each angle of a rectangle is a right angleLet ∠A = 90°. AD ∥ BC, so ∠A + ∠B = 180° and ∠B = 90°. Opposite angles are equal, so ∠C = 90° and ∠D = 90°.
    Example 2. The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicularIn △AOD and △COD: OA = OC (diagonals bisect), OD common, AD = CD. Congruent by SSS, so ∠AOD = ∠COD. They form a linear pair, so each is 90°.
    Example 3. AB = AC, AD bisects exterior ∠PAC, CD ∥ AB: ABCD is a parallelogram∠ABC = ∠ACB. Exterior angle ∠PAC = ∠ABC + ∠ACB = 2∠ACB, and ∠PAC = 2∠DAC. So ∠DAC = ∠ACB (alternate angles), giving BC ∥ AD. With BA ∥ CD (given), ABCD is a parallelogram.
    Example 4. Bisectors of the interior angles between two parallel lines form a rectangle∠PAC = ∠ACR (alternate), so their halves are equal: ∠BAC = ∠ACD, giving AB ∥ DC. Similarly BC ∥ AD, so ABCD is a parallelogram. ∠PAC + ∠CAS = 180°, so ∠BAC + ∠CAD = 90°, i.e. ∠BAD = 90°.
    Example 5. Bisectors of the angles of a parallelogram form a rectangleIn △ASD: ½∠A + ½∠D = ½ × 180° = 90°, so ∠DSA = 90° and ∠PSR = 90° (vertically opposite). Similarly the other three angles are 90°. Opposite angles are equal, so PQRS is a parallelogram with a right angle.
    Example 6. Joining the mid-points D, E, F divides △ABC into four congruent trianglesBy 8.8, DE ∥ AC, DF ∥ BC and EF ∥ AB. So ADEF, BDFE and DFCE are parallelograms. Each has a diagonal that is a side of △DEF, so by 8.1 △DAF, △BDE and △EFC are each congruent to △FED.
    Example 7. Parallel lines l, m, n with AB = BC on p cut DE = EF on qJoin AF, meeting m at G. In △ACF, B is the mid-point of AC and BG ∥ CF, so G is the mid-point of AF (8.9). In △AFD, G is the mid-point of AF and GE ∥ AD, so E is the mid-point of DF.

    Results from Exercises 8.1 and 8.2

    ResultKey steps of the proof
    8.1 Q1. A parallelogram with equal diagonals is a rectangle△ABC ≅ △DCB (SSS: AB = DC, BC common, AC = DB), so ∠ABC = ∠DCB. They are co-interior angles, so they add to 180° and each is 90°.
    8.1 Q2. Diagonals of a square are equal and bisect each other at right angles△ABC ≅ △DCB (SAS) gives AC = BD. A square is a parallelogram, so the diagonals bisect each other. △AOB ≅ △COB (SSS) gives ∠AOB = ∠COB = 90°.
    8.1 Q3. Diagonal AC of a parallelogram bisects ∠A: it bisects ∠C and ABCD is a rhombus∠DAC = ∠BCA and ∠BAC = ∠DCA (alternate angles). Since ∠DAC = ∠BAC, we get ∠BCA = ∠DCA, so AC bisects ∠C. Also ∠BAC = ∠BCA, so AB = BC, and ABCD is a rhombus.
    8.1 Q4. Rectangle in which AC bisects ∠A and ∠C is a square; BD bisects ∠B and ∠DAs in Q3, ∠BAC = ∠BCA, so AB = BC: a rectangle with adjacent sides equal is a square. Then △BAD ≅ △BCD (SSS), so ∠ABD = ∠CBD and ∠ADB = ∠CDB.
    8.1 Q5. P, Q on diagonal BD with DP = BQ: APCQ is a parallelogram△APD ≅ △CQB (SAS: AD = CB, ∠ADP = ∠CBQ, DP = BQ), so AP = CQ. △AQB ≅ △CPD (SAS), so AQ = CP. Both pairs of opposite sides of APCQ are equal (8.3).
    8.1 Q6. AP, CQ perpendicular to BD: △APB ≅ △CQD and AP = CQ∠APB = ∠CQD = 90°, ∠ABP = ∠CDQ (alternate angles), AB = CD. Congruent by AAS; AP = CQ by CPCT.
    8.1 Q7. Trapezium with AB ∥ CD and AD = BC: ∠A = ∠B, ∠C = ∠D, AC = BDDraw CE ∥ DA to meet AB produced at E. AECD is a parallelogram, so CE = AD = BC and ∠CBE = ∠CEB. ∠A + ∠CEB = 180° and ∠ABC + ∠CBE = 180°, so ∠A = ∠B. Then ∠C = 180° – ∠B = 180° – ∠A = ∠D. △ABC ≅ △BAD (SAS), so AC = BD.
    8.2 Q1. Mid-points P, Q, R, S of any quadrilateral form a parallelogramIn △ADC, SR ∥ AC and SR = ½ AC. In △ABC, PQ ∥ AC and PQ = ½ AC. So PQ ∥ SR and PQ = SR.
    8.2 Q2. Mid-points of a rhombus form a rectanglePQRS is a parallelogram (Q1). PQ ∥ AC and QR ∥ BD, and AC ⊥ BD, so ∠PQR = 90°.
    8.2 Q3. Mid-points of a rectangle form a rhombusPQ = SR = ½ AC and QR = PS = ½ BD. In a rectangle AC = BD, so all four sides of PQRS are equal.
    8.2 Q4. Trapezium AB ∥ DC, E mid-point of AD, EF ∥ AB: F is the mid-point of BCLet EF meet BD at G. In △DAB, EG ∥ AB, so G is the mid-point of BD (8.9). In △BCD, GF ∥ DC, so F is the mid-point of BC (8.9).
    8.2 Q5. E, F mid-points of AB, CD in a parallelogram: AF and EC trisect BDAE ∥ FC and AE = FC, so AECF is a parallelogram and AF ∥ EC. In △DQC, FP ∥ CQ, so DP = PQ. In △APB, EQ ∥ AP, so BQ = PQ. Hence DP = PQ = QB.
    8.2 Q6. Right angle at C, M mid-point of AB, MD ∥ BC: D is the mid-point of AC, MD ⊥ AC, CM = MA = ½ ABD is the mid-point of AC (8.9). ∠ADM = ∠ACB = 90° (corresponding angles). △ADM ≅ △CDM (SAS), so CM = AM = ½ AB.

    Quick check

    1. Write the full proof of Theorem 8.3 with a figure, on paper. Which congruence rule does it use?
      Show answer
      SSS, on △ABC and △CDA after drawing the diagonal AC. Then equal alternate angles give the two pairs of parallel sides.
    2. In the proof of Theorem 8.5, where is the angle sum of a quadrilateral used?
      Show answer
      To get 2∠A + 2∠B = 360°, so ∠A + ∠B = 180°. This makes the co-interior angles supplementary, which gives parallel sides.
    3. What construction is used to prove both the Mid-point Theorem and its converse?
      Show answer
      A line through C parallel to BA, meeting the line through E (EF produced) at D. It creates the parallelogram BCDE and the congruent triangles AEF and CDF.
    4. ★ Without looking, prove that the bisectors of the angles of a parallelogram form a rectangle.
      Show answer
      See Example 5 in the table: show one angle is 90° using ½∠A + ½∠D = 90° in △ASD, repeat for the other three, then conclude parallelogram with a right angle.

    ✓Common mistakes to avoid

    • 'Bisect each other' is not 'equal'. The diagonals of every parallelogram bisect each other, but they are equal only in a rectangle (or square).
    • Diagonals bisect the vertex angles only in a rhombus or square, not in a general parallelogram or rectangle.
    • Adjacent angles add to 180°; opposite angles are equal. Do not mix these up.
    • Naming order matters. In quadrilateral ABCD the sides are AB, BC, CD, DA and the diagonals are AC and BD.
    • Mid-point Theorem needs mid-points of two sides. The segment is half of the third side, the one it does not touch.
    • Use the converse correctly: a line through one mid-point parallel to a side bisects the other side. You must know it is parallel first.
    • Mid-points of a rectangle give a rhombus, and of a rhombus give a rectangle. Students often swap these.
    • In a rhombus, use half-diagonals with Pythagoras to find the side: side2 = (d1/2)2 + (d2/2)2.
    • Give a reason for every step of a proof: 'alternate angles', 'CPCT', 'Theorem 8.8', and so on.
