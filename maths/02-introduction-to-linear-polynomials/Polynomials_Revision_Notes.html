    01Polynomials and their vocabulary

    A polynomial in one variable is an algebraic expression in which every power of the variable is a whole number (0, 1, 2, 3, ...). The coefficients can be any real numbers.

    ExpressionPolynomial?Reason
    x3 – x2 + 4x + 7YesPowers 3, 2, 1, 0 are whole numbers
    √2 x2 + πYesThe coefficients may be irrational; the powers are whole numbers
    x + 1/xNo1/x = x–1; the power –1 is not a whole number
    √x + 3No√x = x1/2; the power ½ is not a whole number
    x10 + y3 + t50Not in one variableIt has three variables
    • Terms and coefficients: in –x3 + 4x2 + 7x – 2 the terms are –x3, 4x2, 7x and –2. The coefficient of x3 is –1, of x2 is 4, of x is 7, and –2 is the constant term (the coefficient of x0).
    • By number of terms: monomial (1 term), binomial (2 terms), trinomial (3 terms).
    • Degree: the highest power of the variable. The degree of 3x7 – 4x6 + x + 9 is 7.
    • Constant polynomials: 2, –5, 7. A non-zero constant has degree 0 (since 2 = 2x0).
    • Zero polynomial: the constant polynomial 0. Its degree is not defined.
    DegreeNameGeneral formMost terms it can have
    1Linearax + b, a ≠ 02
    2Quadraticax2 + bx + c, a ≠ 03
    3Cubicax3 + bx2 + cx + d, a ≠ 04

    A polynomial of degree n has the form anxn + an–1xn–1 + ... + a1x + a0, where an ≠ 0. We name polynomials p(x), q(x), r(t) and so on.

    Quick check

    1. Which of these are polynomials in one variable? (i) 3x2 – √5 x + 1   (ii) 2/x + x   (iii) y3 – 4   (iv) √t + t
      Show answer
      (i) and (iii). In (ii) the power of x is –1, and in (iv) it is ½.
    2. Write the degree of 7x3 – (π/2)x2 + 4 and the coefficient of x2.
      Show answer
      Degree 3; the coefficient of x2 is –π/2.
    3. Classify 5 – t2 by its number of terms and by its degree.
      Show answer
      A binomial; quadratic (degree 2).
    4. What are the degrees of the polynomials 9 and 0?
      Show answer
      9 has degree 0. The degree of the zero polynomial 0 is not defined.
    5. ★ p(x) has degree 2 and q(x) has degree 3. What is the degree of p(x) × q(x)? Can p(x) + q(x) have degree 2?
      Show answer
      The product has degree 2 + 3 = 5. The sum always has degree 3, because nothing in p(x) can cancel the x3 term of q(x).

    02Value of a polynomial and its zeroes

    The value of p(x) at x = a is found by replacing x with a. It is written p(a).

    Example. For p(x) = 5x3 – 2x2 + 3x – 2: p(1) = 5 – 2 + 3 – 2 = 4 and p(0) = –2.
    c is a zero of p(x) when p(c) = 0c is then also called a root of the equation p(x) = 0
    • Linear polynomial ax + b: exactly one zero, x = –b/a. The zero of 2x + 1 is –1/2.
    • Non-zero constant polynomial (such as 5): no zero at all.
    • Zero polynomial: every real number is a zero.
    • A zero of a polynomial need not be 0, and 0 may be a zero. x2 – 2x has the zeroes 0 and 2.
    • A polynomial can have more than one zero.

    To check whether a number is a zero, substitute it and see whether the value is 0. To find the zero of a linear polynomial, solve p(x) = 0.

    Quick check

    1. For p(x) = 2x3 – x2 + 3, find p(–1). What does the answer tell you?
      Show answer
      p(–1) = –2 – 1 + 3 = 0, so –1 is a zero of p(x).
    2. Find the zero of 4x – 7.
      Show answer
      4x – 7 = 0 gives x = 7/4.
    3. Are 1 and –2 zeroes of x2 + x – 2?
      Show answer
      Yes: 1 + 1 – 2 = 0 and 4 – 2 – 2 = 0.
    4. How many zeroes does the constant polynomial 9 have?
      Show answer
      None. Its value is 9 for every x.
    5. ★ If 2 is a zero of p(x) = x2 – kx + 6, find k and the other zero.
      Show answer
      p(2) = 4 – 2k + 6 = 0 gives k = 5. Then x2 – 5x + 6 = (x – 2)(x – 3), so the other zero is 3.
    Got all 5 right?

    03The Factor Theorem

    x – a is a factor of p(x)  ⇔  p(a) = 0for a polynomial p(x) of degree 1 or more and any real number a

    Why it works: dividing p(x) by x – a gives p(x) = (x – a) q(x) + p(a). If p(a) = 0, then p(x) = (x – a) q(x), so x – a is a factor. If x – a is a factor, then p(a) = (a – a) q(a) = 0.

    To test this factorFind its zeroCheck
    x – ax = ap(a) = 0
    x + ax = –ap(–a) = 0
    ax + bx = –b/ap(–b/a) = 0
    Example 1. Is x + 2 a factor of x3 + 3x2 + 5x + 6? p(–2) = –8 + 12 – 10 + 6 = 0. Yes.
    Example 2 (finding k). x – 1 is a factor of 4x3 + 3x2 – 4x + k. So p(1) = 4 + 3 – 4 + k = 0, giving k = –3.

    Quick check

    1. Is x + 1 a factor of x3 + x2 + x + 1?
      Show answer
      p(–1) = –1 + 1 – 1 + 1 = 0. Yes.
    2. Find k if x – 2 is a factor of x2 – kx + 6.
      Show answer
      p(2) = 4 – 2k + 6 = 0, so k = 5.
    3. Is 2x + 1 a factor of 2x2 + 7x + 3?
      Show answer
      p(–1/2) = 1/2 – 7/2 + 3 = 0. Yes.
    4. ★ Both x – 1 and x + 1 are factors of x3 + ax2 + bx – 2. Find a and b.
      Show answer
      p(1) = 0 gives a + b = 1. p(–1) = 0 gives a – b = 3. So a = 2 and b = –1. Check: x3 + 2x2 – x – 2 = (x2 – 1)(x + 2).
    5. ★ If x – k is a factor of x3 – kx2 + 2x – 6, find k.
      Show answer
      p(k) = k3 – k3 + 2k – 6 = 0, so k = 3.
    Got all 5 right?

    04Factorising quadratics and cubics

    Quadratics: splitting the middle term

    To factorise ax2 + bx + c, find two numbers whose sum is b and whose product is ac. Split bx using them, then group.

    Example. 6x2 + 17x + 5: sum 17, product 6 × 5 = 30. The numbers are 2 and 15.
    6x2 + 2x + 15x + 5 = 2x(3x + 1) + 5(3x + 1) = (3x + 1)(2x + 5).

    Cubics: find one factor, then finish

    1. Try the factors of the constant term (±1, ±2, ...) until one gives p(a) = 0. Then x – a is a factor.
    2. Take out (x – a) by regrouping the terms (or by dividing).
    3. Factorise the quadratic that is left by splitting its middle term.
    Example. p(x) = x3 – 2x2 – 5x + 6. Trying x = 1: p(1) = 1 – 2 – 5 + 6 = 0, so x – 1 is a factor.
    x3 – x2 – x2 + x – 6x + 6 = x2(x – 1) – x(x – 1) – 6(x – 1) = (x – 1)(x2 – x – 6) = (x – 1)(x – 3)(x + 2).

    Check your answer: the constant terms of the factors must multiply to give the constant term of the polynomial. Here (–1)(–3)(2) = 6.

    Quick check

    1. Factorise 2x2 + 7x + 3.
      Show answer
      Sum 7, product 6: use 6 and 1. (2x + 1)(x + 3).
    2. Factorise 3x2 – x – 4.
      Show answer
      Sum –1, product –12: use –4 and 3. (3x – 4)(x + 1).
    3. Factorise x3 + 2x2 – x – 2.
      Show answer
      x2(x + 2) – (x + 2) = (x + 2)(x – 1)(x + 1).
    4. ★ Factorise x3 – 7x + 6 and write its zeroes.
      Show answer
      p(1) = 0. (x – 1)(x2 + x – 6) = (x – 1)(x – 2)(x + 3). Zeroes: 1, 2 and –3.
    5. ★ The area of a rectangle is 25a2 – 35a + 12. Give possible expressions for its length and breadth.
      Show answer
      Sum –35, product 300: use –20 and –15. (5a – 4)(5a – 3).
    Got all 5 right?

    05Algebraic identities

    An identity is true for all values of its variables. Learn these eight, with their signs.

    No.Identity
    I(x + y)2 = x2 + 2xy + y2
    II(x – y)2 = x2 – 2xy + y2
    IIIx2 – y2 = (x + y)(x – y)
    IV(x + a)(x + b) = x2 + (a + b)x + ab
    V(x + y + z)2 = x2 + y2 + z2 + 2xy + 2yz + 2zx
    VI(x + y)3 = x3 + y3 + 3xy(x + y) = x3 + 3x2y + 3xy2 + y3
    VII(x – y)3 = x3 – y3 – 3xy(x – y) = x3 – 3x2y + 3xy2 – y3
    VIIIx3 + y3 + z3 – 3xyz = (x + y + z)(x2 + y2 + z2 – xy – yz – zx)

    Results that follow from them

    x3 + y3 = (x + y)(x2 – xy + y2)
    x3 – y3 = (x – y)(x2 + xy + y2)
    If x + y + z = 0, then x3 + y3 + z3 = 3xyz
    x3 + y3 + z3 – 3xyz = ½ (x + y + z)[(x – y)2 + (y – z)2 + (z – x)2]

    How they are used

    • Quick arithmetic: 105 × 106 = (100 + 5)(100 + 6) = 10000 + 1100 + 30 = 11130. Also 1043 = (100 + 4)3 = 1000000 + 64 + 3(100)(4)(104) = 1124864.
    • Expanding with negative terms: treat a minus sign as part of the term. (4a – 2b – 3c)2 = [4a + (–2b) + (–3c)]2 = 16a2 + 4b2 + 9c2 – 16ab + 12bc – 24ac.
    • Factorising by spotting a pattern: 8x3 + 27y3 + 36x2y + 54xy2 = (2x)3 + (3y)3 + 3(2x)(3y)(2x + 3y) = (2x + 3y)3.
    • Sum of cubes without cubing: (–12)3 + 73 + 53. Since –12 + 7 + 5 = 0, the value is 3(–12)(7)(5) = –1260.
    • Finding one quantity from others: a2 + b2 + c2 = (a + b + c)2 – 2(ab + bc + ca), and x3 + y3 = (x + y)3 – 3xy(x + y).

    Quick check

    1. Evaluate 97 × 103 without multiplying directly.
      Show answer
      (100 – 3)(100 + 3) = 10000 – 9 = 9991.
    2. Expand (2x – y + 3z)2.
      Show answer
      4x2 + y2 + 9z2 – 4xy – 6yz + 12xz.
    3. Expand (2a + 3b)3.
      Show answer
      8a3 + 36a2b + 54ab2 + 27b3.
    4. Factorise 27x3 – 8y3.
      Show answer
      (3x – 2y)(9x2 + 6xy + 4y2).
    5. Factorise 8x3 + y3 + 27z3 – 18xyz.
      Show answer
      (2x + y + 3z)(4x2 + y2 + 9z2 – 2xy – 3yz – 6xz).
    6. ★ If a + b + c = 6 and ab + bc + ca = 11, find a2 + b2 + c2.
      Show answer
      36 – 2(11) = 14.
    7. ★ If x + y = 5 and xy = 6, find x3 + y3.
      Show answer
      53 – 3(6)(5) = 125 – 90 = 35.
    8. ★ Real numbers a, b, c satisfy a2 + b2 + c2 = ab + bc + ca. What can you say about them?
      Show answer
      ½[(a – b)2 + (b – c)2 + (c – a)2] = 0. A sum of squares is zero only when each square is zero, so a = b = c.

    ✓Common mistakes to avoid

    • Whole-number powers only. 1/x and √x make an expression a non-polynomial, but an irrational coefficient such as √2 does not.
    • Degree of constants: a non-zero constant has degree 0; the zero polynomial has no defined degree.
    • Sign in the Factor Theorem: for the factor x + 2 substitute x = –2, not 2.
    • Splitting the middle term: the two numbers multiply to give a × c, not just c.
    • (x + y)2 is not x2 + y2, and (x + y)3 is not x3 + y3. Do not drop the middle terms.
    • Signs in x3 ± y3: the middle sign in the second bracket is the opposite of the sign in the first bracket.
    • x3 + y3 + z3 = 3xyz holds only when x + y + z = 0. Check the sum first.
    • Negative terms in Identity V: a product of two negative terms gives a positive term, e.g. 2(–2b)(–3c) = +12bc.
    • A zero of a polynomial need not be 0. 'Zero of p(x)' means a value of x that makes p(x) = 0.
